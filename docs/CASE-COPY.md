## 01 · Lex Energy

**Meta line** Brand · Website

**Index one-liner** Kerbside EV charging. A new category, and nobody had heard of it.

**Standfirst** Off-peak electricity, stored at the kerb, sold to drivers at any hour.

**Body** New proposition, no name recognition, and an idea that had to land before anyone read a word about it. The identity does the explaining. Brand and website were built together so they said the same thing.

**Against the package**

| Item | Copy |
|---|---|
| Brand direction | Credible rebel. A challenger position, written down before any design started. |
| Logo | A dot-matrix lockup that reads as energy and infrastructure at once. Legible on a kerbside unit and at favicon size. |
| Typography | A mono family. Technical, even, and readable at distance. |
| Colour palette | Lex Lime against black and grey. One loud colour, rationed. |
| Imagery direction | City photography with the identity laid over the top, never inside it. |
| Tone of voice | Short, technical, unimpressed. |
| **Website** *(beyond base package)* | One scrollable site built off the same system. Desktop and mobile. |

## 02 · Plintle

**Meta line** Brand · Website · Naming

**Index one-liner** A subtitling giant's consumer spin-off. The name came with the job.

**Standfirst** Europe's largest content localisation company, launching direct to consumers.

**Body** Plintle subtitles and dubs for Netflix, HBO, Warner Brothers and Universal. The consumer product had to stand on its own, away from the parent business. It did not have a name when the work started.

**Against the package**

| Item | Copy |
|---|---|
| Brand direction | Simple, in a category that is anything but. |
| Logo | A wordmark plus a p monogram that works alone as an avatar. |
| Typography | One family, with a written type guide showing how it gets set. |
| Colour palette | Six colours in a dot system. Bright without being loud. |
| Imagery direction | Shape and pattern instead of photography. Cheaper to keep consistent, and it never runs out. |
| Tone of voice | Subtitling made simple. Short sentences, no jargon, no boasting. |
| **Naming** *(beyond base package)* | Plintle did not exist before this. Named as part of the job. |
| **Website** *(beyond base package)* | Home, product, pricing and sign-up, built off the same system. |

## 04 · Eddy

**Meta line** Brand

**Index one-liner** Process software. Complicated to use, simple to look at.

**Standfirst** A coordination tool that builds process flows across separate digital touchpoints.

**Body** A genuinely complicated service that needed a brand simple enough to make it look easy. Six words defined the whole system before a single mark was drawn.

**Against the package**

| Item | Copy |
|---|---|
| Brand direction | Efficient, adaptable, dependable, resourceful, impactful, open. Six words, agreed first. |
| Logo | One mark built from a loop. Wordmark, app icon and favicon out of the same shape. |
| Typography | Urbanist. Six weights, one family, no exceptions. |
| Colour palette | Blue, pink and cream. Warm enough to soften a piece of process software. |
| Imagery direction | Product screens shot plainly. The interface is the hero. |
| Tone of voice | Process to the people. Plain language for a technical product. |

## 05 · 90110

**Meta line** Brand

**Index one-liner** The fourth emergency service. Everything that is not a 112 call.

**Standfirst** Help for the emergencies that are not emergencies.

**Body** A fallen tree across the drive. Three feet of snow on the street. Nobody obvious to call. 90110 had to make an unfamiliar number feel as automatic as the three that came before it.

**Against the package**

| Item | Copy |
|---|---|
| Brand direction | A written manifesto. Consider it done. |
| Logo | A shield mark that holds at any size, from a van door to a phone screen. |
| Typography | Manuka. Solid and bold. Chosen to behave like the service. |
| Colour palette | Six steps of near-black to light grey, numbered and fixed. |
| Imagery direction | Night, weather, and the moment before help arrives. |
| Tone of voice | Blunt, short, no hedging. The brand prints its own rule on the wall. |
