Process

How it works.

Standard Work is designed to be calm and predictable. No workshops. No discovery phases. No open-ended processes.

Everything is defined before work begins.

Ready to start?

Five steps

01Choose what you need

Start with the brand, or go straight to a website if you already have one. You'll see the full price and scope immediately.

02Answer a few practical questions

You'll be guided through a short configuration flow. Defaults are provided. You only need to adjust what matters. This usually takes a few minutes.

03Review and confirm

Before confirming, you'll see everything clearly:

  • What's included
  • What's not included
  • The total cost
  • Delivery timing

Payment: 50% to start, 50% on final delivery.

04We do the work

Once confirmed, we review your submission and begin. The work is developed using a structured system and reviewed by experienced designers. No meetings required unless needed.

05Review and delivery

You'll receive a draft to review. One revision round is included to refine the work within the agreed scope. Final files are delivered once the remaining balance is paid.

Harry da BBQ.

A BBQ smokehouse on Sweden's wild west coast, going bigger.

Harryda BBQ was a delicious secret: a smoker deep in the forest producing meat worth driving for. Two problems. Outside Sweden the name was unsayable. And BBQ is a loud category to be quiet in.

01Brand direction

The Manifeasto. If it isn't smoked, smoke it. If it's smoked, eat it. If it tastes good, repeat it.

02Logo

A wordmark built to be read through smoke and across a car park. Holds on a shutter, an apron and a van door.

03Typography

Raleway Black Italic for the shouting, Shrikhand for the accents. Two faces, one job each.

04Colour palette

Perky Pink, Ruby Red, Bark Black and Smokey Smoke. Named so they get used properly.

05Imagery direction

Smoke, and people in it. Food shot close and hot, never styled cold.

06Tone of voice

Sassy and succulent. Serious about the meat, never solemn about it.

07NamingBeyond the base package

Harryda became Harry da BBQ. One pun, and the name works in every language it needs to.

08MerchandiseBeyond the base package

Aprons and tees for the crew, built off the same wordmark.

09Signage and liveryBeyond the base package

The container, the shutter and the van. All pink, all unmissable.

Eddy.

A coordination tool that builds process flows across separate digital touchpoints.

A genuinely complicated service that needed a brand simple enough to make it look easy. Six words defined the whole system before a single mark was drawn.

01Brand direction

Efficient, adaptable, dependable, resourceful, impactful, open. Six words, agreed first.

02Logo

One mark built from a loop. Wordmark, app icon and favicon out of the same shape.

03Typography

Urbanist. Six weights, one family, no exceptions.

04Colour palette

Blue, pink and cream. Warm enough to soften a piece of process software.

05Imagery direction

Product screens shot plainly. The interface is the hero.

06Tone of voice

Process to the people. Plain language for a technical product.

07MerchandiseBeyond the base package

A tee and a hoodie carrying the mark on its own. The loop holds at chest size with no wordmark under it.

Lex Energy.

Off-peak electricity, stored at the kerb, sold to drivers at any hour.

New proposition, no name recognition, and an idea that had to land before anyone read a word about it. The identity does the explaining. Brand and website were built together so they said the same thing.

01Brand direction

Credible rebel. A challenger position, written down before any design started.

02Logo

A dot-matrix lockup that reads as energy and infrastructure at once. Legible on a kerbside unit and at favicon size.

03Typography

Future for headlines and subheads, Urbanist for everything else. Technical, even, readable at distance.

04Colour palette

Lex Lime against black and grey. One loud colour, rationed.

05Imagery direction

City photography with the identity laid over the top, never inside it.

06Tone of voice

Short, technical, unimpressed.

07MerchandiseBeyond the base package

A tee and a shell jacket. The dot-matrix mark at chest size and at cuff size.

08Vehicle liveryBeyond the base package

The mark run as a dot field down the flank. Lex Lime doing the work at forty metres.

Sebratec.

Engineering and technology problem-solving for industry.

Competent, credible, and impossible to tell apart from the twelve other results on the same search page. The work gave them a position and a system that holds it.

01Brand direction

Human, in a category that reads like a spec sheet.

02Logo

A lowercase wordmark. Warm, plain, set in one weight.

03Typography

Il Vorkurs. Bold, Medium and Light. One family across everything, from a proposal to a phone screen.

04Colour palette

Six named colours from Aubergine to Tang, with a gradient built into the system.

05Imagery direction

Soft three-dimensional forms and a dot pattern. Both made in-house, so the supply never ends.

06Tone of voice

We are human and we speak as humans do. Written as a rule, not a slogan.

07MerchandiseBeyond the base package

Outerwear in Aubergine and Cerise, with the s monogram. Cold-weather kit for consultants on someone else's site.
